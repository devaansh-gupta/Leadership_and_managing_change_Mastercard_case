Core case problem · 2021

How can Mastercard stay indispensable if the future of payments moves beyond cards—and beyond Mastercard’s own network?

Mastercard was financially strong, but the rules of competition were shifting. Wallets could control the customer interface, national instant-payment systems could bypass card rails, and fintechs could specialise in layers of the value chain. Miebach had to protect the profitable core while making the company relevant across new ways of moving money.

CHAPTER 01 · THE TENSION

A strong card network, a narrow definition of competition

Mastercard had scale, trusted bank relationships and a valuable global brand. But the business was still closely tied to card transactions. With a large share of global transactions still in cash, the bigger opportunity was also a warning: digital payments could grow through channels Mastercard did not own.

What this meansThe central problem was not poor performance. It was the risk that the basis of competition would change faster than the organisation.

✓ Where Mastercard was ahead by 2021

Revenue had grown from $5.54B in 2010 to $15.30B in 2020; net income rose from $1.85B to $6.41B. Services had expanded from 8% of revenue in 2008 to roughly one-third by 2021. Acquisitions and partnerships had built capabilities in analytics, security, identity, real-time payments and open banking.

! What was still unresolved

The case does not end with a final victory or a completed transformation. Miebach had to decide how Mastercard would remain relevant as wallets, national rails, fintechs, alternative currencies and data-sovereignty requirements reshaped the industry. The achievement was a stronger starting position—not immunity from disruption.

MISALIGNMENT
Decision room · CEO Michael Miebach · 2021

What should Mastercard do when the industry no longer guarantees that every payment runs on a card?

Recommendation: preserve the core, diversify the economics, and become a trusted technology and services layer across payment methods. Do not treat this as a choice between defending cards and embracing disruption—Mastercard needs both.

CEO action agenda

Six moves, in the right order

RECOMMENDED
01
Protect the core

Modernise cards, acceptance and network trust

Maintain reliability, transaction performance, fraud controls and issuer/merchant relationships. Avoid defending share through price discounting alone.

02
Diversify the economics

Scale services with clear customer value

Link identity, cyber, analytics, fraud prevention, loyalty and open banking to customer problems and demonstrable willingness to pay.

03
Be rail-agnostic

Partner with national and account-to-account rails

Offer interoperability, security, identity and cross-border capabilities where Mastercard is additive—not just where it can route a card.

04
Curate the ecosystem

Partner, build or buy—with discipline

Use partner distribution where it accelerates scale; build differentiated trust capabilities; acquire only for strategic capability, with integration metrics.

05
Place selective bets

Explore crypto and CBDCs without betting the firm

Run compliant pilots, define stop/go criteria, and treat uncertain technologies as options until customer demand and economics validate them.

06
Align the organisation

Use 7S to make change operational

Align structure, systems, leadership, staff, skills and shared values with Grow–Diversify–Build. Keep global security standards with local responsiveness.
